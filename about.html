The V.E.G.A.S. Cooldown Benchmark

The V.E.G.A.S. Cooldown Benchmark is a scoring system built for players who are tired of buying hype and ending up with heartbreak. It rates games across five pillars: Values, Ethics, Gameplay, Accessibility, and Standards.

Where most reviews score games based on subjective values like graphics and gameplay mechanics, the V.E.G.A.S. Benchmark stays objective and asks whether the studio actually deserved your money. Points are awarded and deducted based on real, documented game launch and life conditions. No vibes, no nostalgia, no hype cycles.

We only score games that are at least 3 years old — this keeps us away from hype cycles and gives you a fair, honest and definitive verdict on whether a game and studio actually earned their score.

Values

25 points

Launch price, monetization, early access, and included features.

Day 1 Launch Value

  • Budget Kings Games launched with love and soul, not greed and pipe dreams. These games cost less than the average price of similar games released the same year. +5 points
  • Take My Money Games that came out strong but totally worth every penny. These games came out at the average price point of other similar games released that year. +3 points
  • Greed Tax Games that came out costing a pretty penny without really anything interesting to show. More expensive than other similar games released that year. -5 points
  • Whale Fishing Games that came out over priced, either deserved or not, but way out of line with other games release the same year. -20 points

Demos & Early Access

  • Free Deals Games that came out with a free demo or an open beta or an early access. They didn't have to do it, yet they still did it, and for free. +5 points
  • Beta Cover Charge Games that came out with a paid beta under $20. Not generous, not outrageous, just an early look with a small cover charge. 0 points
  • Semi Greedy Hype Games that came out with a demo or an early access version (non multi player game), but at a price. Even before launch the game studio wanted your money. -3 points
  • The Nerve Games with an early access version that IS multiplayer, giving a headstart to only players who fork out MORE of their cash! SHAME on the developer! -20 points

Monetization (Microtransactions)

  • Clean slate Games that came out with ZERO microtransactions. The game was the retail price with no additional costs. +5 points
  • Minor MTX Games that came out with some microtransactions, but no individual MTX exceeds $5. 0 points
  • Moderate MTX Games that came out with some microtransactions, but no individual MTX exceeds $20. -3 points
  • Extreme MTX Games that came out with microtransactions that cost more than $50. Scandalous! -10 points

Loot Boxes & Random Rewards

  • No Gamble Boxes Games that came out with ZERO loot boxes or random rewards purchasable with real money. No slot-machine nonsense hiding inside the purchase. +5 points
  • Transparent Trinkets Games with cosmetic-only random rewards, transparent odds, and a single tradeable safety-currency. Still random, but at least the rules are on the table. 0 points
  • Casino Lite Games with casino-like random mechanics using a single tradeable currency system. One layer of funny money is already one layer too many. -5 points
  • Currency Maze Games with casino-like random mechanics using multiple tradeable currency systems. Confusing by design, expensive by outcome. -10 points

Subscriptions & Battle Pass

  • Don't want no sub Games that came out with ZERO subscription or recurring cost of any kind. The game was bought once with no other fees. +5 points
  • Cosmetic Pass Games with a cosmetic-only, single-tier pass with no gameplay benefits. 0 points
  • Premium Pass Games that came out with a premium, multi-tier battle pass or season pass that includes gameplay benefits. -5 points

In-game Advertisements

  • No Billboard Nonsense Games that came out with no in-game advertisement screens, popups, or flashing menu bait. Nothing extra earned, but nothing annoying shoved in your face either. 0 points
  • Ad Bait Games that came out with optional ads for minor rewards or temporarily flashing/highlighted menus. Technically optional, still tacky. -3 points
  • Billboard Tax Games that came out with forced ads or disruptive ad placements, including persistent flashing/highlighted elements. You paid for a game, not a billboard. -5 points

Ethics

25 points

Kudos for honest marketing and long-term developer support over the years.

Marketing & Communication

  • Perfect Marketing Games that came out within 18 months of initial announcement with no fake promises, with multiple trailers and no push backed dates. They were organized enough to avoid the infamous "crunch-time" and delivered on time! Major Kudos!. +5 points
  • Long Announcement Games that came out between 18 and 24 months of initial announcement. Extended marketing window but manageable hype. 0 points
  • Extended Pipe-dream Games that came out more than 24 months after announcement! This is just terrible marketing that tests player patience far too long. -10 points

Post-launch Support

  • Iron Man Games that came out and received FREE continued support and content updates for over 2 years after initial launch. This was a labor of love from the devs recognized by the players. +10 points
  • Solid Support Games that came out and received FREE continued support for between 1 and 2 years after initial launch. A solid commitment to the community. +5 points
  • Abandoned Games that came out with support lasting less than 1 year after launch, then left to stagnate. Disappointing given how quickly they moved on. -10 points

Truthfulness

  • Feats First Games that came out all the features mentioned during the marketing period prior to launch. Nothing was cut from the release. +10 points
  • Wait a minute... Games that came out with at least 1 missing feature without comment or clarification. -3 points
  • Rip Off Games that came out with at least multiple missing features OR removed negative comments about the game after launch. -10 points

Gameplay

10 points

Rewards gameplay depth, campaign length, and player choice.

Campaign Length

  • The Long Haul Games with a massive 20+ hour main campaign. Plenty of bang for your buck! +5 points
  • Decent Length Games with a solid 15-20 hour main campaign. A respectable length for most adventures. +3 points
  • Moderate Length Games with a campaign between 5-15 hours. A reasonable middle ground experience. 0 points
  • Wait, it's over? Games with less than a 5 hour campaign. It feels more like a demo than a full game. -5 points

Replayability

  • Multiple Endings Games that offer multiple endings or diverse paths to completion based on player choices (different classes, characters, story branches). +5 points
  • Single Focus Games with only one ending or a pure sandbox with no specific endpoint. Story is linear but well-crafted. +3 points

Accessibility

10 points

Rates accessibility in input controller support, display settings, and language localization.

Input & Controls

  • Multiple Inputs Support for multiple standard input methods natively, such as both Keyboard & Mouse AND Controller. This allows players to customize their experience to their preferences. +2 points
  • Single Input Only one input method supported (e.g., Keyboard & Mouse ONLY or Controller ONLY). Players are limited in control options.0 points

Visuals & Ease of Use

  • Inclusive Design Comprehensive accessibility options included such as colorblind modes, subtitles, and difficulty changes to ensure more people can enjoy the game. +2 points
  • Barriers to Entry Zero accessibility options provided to assist players with different visual or cognitive needs. One size fits few. Disappointing approach.-3 points

Localization

  • Global Citizen The game is fully translated into at least 3 different languages with localized text, subtitles, and voiceovers, making it accessible to a broader audience. The devs didn't have to do this. It took time and extra effort. Kudos!+6 points
  • Language Locked Only one language available at launch, limiting the audience to a single linguistic group. Limited global reach.0 points

Standards

30 points

Measures technical performance, community freedom, and launch reception.

Connectivity & Longevity

  • Solo Power Games that are fully playable offline independently of remote servers or online accounts. You own what you bought. +5 points
  • Partial Offline Games that can be played partially offline (solo campaign only) while multiplayer requires connection. 0 points
  • Plug Pulled The ultimate failure. The game is dead, no longer supported, and cannot be played at all. -100 points

Cross-Platform & Community

  • Single-Player Exemption Games that are primarily single-player automatically receive full credit as cross-platform requirements do not apply to single-player experiences. +10 points
  • Ultimate Bridge For multiplayer games: Full support for cross-platform play across 3 or more major platforms at launch (e.g., PC, Xbox, PlayStation). +10 points
  • Dual Bridge For multiplayer games: Cross-platform play between 2 major platforms at launch. +3 points
  • Platform Isolated For multiplayer games: No cross-platform play support at launch. 0 points

Modding Support

  • Modder's Paradise Full, open modding support provided with official tools and documentation, allowing the community to thrive. +5 points
  • No Modding No mod support or very limited support provided. 0 points
  • Creative Ban Developers actively ban or restrict modding, stifling community creativity and game longevity. -5 points

File Size Optimization

  • Lean Machine Game file size is average or below average for its generation compared to similar games of the same genre released the same year. +5 points
  • Reasonable Size Slightly higher than average, but less than double the genre average for the generation. 0 points
  • Storage Hog Disproportionate file size, more than double the typical industry average for the genre. Excessive bloat. -5 points

Technical Health & Reception

  • Critical Darling Positive initial reviews from players and critics (at least 4/5 or 80%+ based on a combination of scores from Metacritic, Steam reviews, GameSpot, and IGN user scores). +5 points
  • Mixed Reception Mixed reviews from players (2.5 to 3/5 on Backloggd, 5 to 7/10 on Metacritic User Score, or Steam Mixed rating). Decent but with concerns. -2 points
  • Total Trainwreck Below 2.5/5 or 50% average player reviews at launch (based on same sources as cited earlier). A technical or conceptual disaster. -20 points
